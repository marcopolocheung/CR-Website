# China Rose Legal And Launch Checklist

This is a practical pre-launch checklist for a static U.S. restaurant website, not jurisdiction-specific legal advice.

### Accessibility — highest priority

The Justice Department considers websites of businesses open to the public subject to the ADA’s accessibility requirements. It does not prescribe one detailed technical standard for private businesses, but points businesses toward standards such as WCAG. ([ADA.gov][1])

* [ ] The entire site can be navigated using only the keyboard.
* [ ] A visible focus indicator shows which link or button is selected.
* [ ] Text has sufficient contrast against its background.
* [ ] Text remains usable when the browser is zoomed to 200%.
* [ ] Every meaningful image has accurate alternative text.
* [ ] Decorative images use empty alternative text so screen readers ignore them.
* [ ] Headings follow a sensible order: one main heading, then subsections.
* [ ] Links have descriptive names such as “Order pickup from North Austin,” not just “Click here.”
* [ ] Each location’s address, phone number and hours appear as selectable text—not only inside a map or image.
* [ ] The two locations are clearly distinguished everywhere.
* [ ] The menu is written in HTML whenever possible.
* [ ] If there is a PDF menu, it is accessible and the essential information is also available on the page.
* [ ] There is no important information communicated only through color.
* [ ] The mobile version is readable and buttons are easy to press.
* [ ] The site has been tested with a basic accessibility scanner such as Lighthouse or WAVE.
* [ ] Someone has manually tested keyboard navigation; automated scanners alone are not enough.
* [ ] A customer can call the restaurant if the Toast or Uber Eats ordering process is inaccessible.

### Menu and business information

Online statements and advertisements must be truthful and not misleading. That means the practical concern is keeping what you publish accurate—not adding dietary statements you are not making. ([Federal Trade Commission][2])

* [ ] Menu item names and descriptions are accurate.
* [ ] Displayed prices are current.
* [ ] Hours are current for both locations.
* [ ] Addresses and phone numbers are correct.
* [ ] Holiday or temporary hours are not presented as permanent hours.
* [ ] Toast and Uber Eats links lead to the correct restaurant location.
* [ ] Pickup and delivery buttons clearly identify which service they use.
* [ ] Any promotions, discounts or specials include relevant conditions and expiration dates.
* [ ] The site does not claim food is halal, kosher, gluten-free, vegan, organic, locally sourced or allergen-safe unless the restaurant can support that claim.
* [ ] If prices differ between locations or ordering platforms, the site says so.

A simple menu note is reasonable:

> Menu items, prices and availability may vary by location and ordering platform and are subject to change.

You do **not** need to add dietary or allergy claims merely because the menu contains chicken, beef or other ingredients.

### Careers page

* [ ] Remove the careers link until it works; **or**
* [ ] Replace the broken page with: “We are not currently accepting applications.”
* [ ] Do not advertise specific openings that do not currently exist.
* [ ] Make the application process accessible once hiring is enabled.
* [ ] Do not collect résumés or applicant information until there is a secure and intentional process for handling it.

A dead careers link is primarily a maintenance and professionalism problem, but leaving misleading job information up should be avoided.

### Photos, logo, fonts and other content

Photographs are generally owned by the photographer—not automatically by the restaurant shown in the photograph. Permission is therefore needed for photos taken by contractors, customers or other third parties. ([U.S. Copyright Office][3])

* [ ] The restaurant owns or has permission to use every photograph.
* [ ] No images were copied from Google Images, Yelp, Instagram or another restaurant’s website without permission.
* [ ] The restaurant owns or is authorized to use its logo.
* [ ] Website fonts are properly licensed or obtained from a service that permits web use.
* [ ] Icons and illustrations are original, licensed or from a library that permits commercial use.
* [ ] Customer photographs or testimonials are used only with permission.
* [ ] You have written confirmation from the restaurant owner that supplied content may be published.

### Map and external services

* [ ] Use the official map embed—not a copied screenshot with attribution removed.
* [ ] Check whether the embedded map loads third-party cookies or tracking requests.
* [ ] If you want the site to remain as privacy-minimal as possible, replace the embedded map with a normal “View on Google Maps” link.
* [ ] Make sure Toast and Uber Eats links are clearly third-party ordering links.
* [ ] Do not imply that the restaurant controls delivery fees, platform availability or the delivery service’s privacy practices.
* [ ] Periodically test every external link.

### Privacy

For a site with **no analytics, advertising pixels, forms, accounts, newsletter signup or onsite ordering**, you generally do not need a complicated privacy policy or cookie-consent banner.

Still verify the following:

* [ ] No Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, TikTok Pixel or similar code is installed.
* [ ] No hidden contact form or email signup collects information.
* [ ] No third-party chat widget is running.
* [ ] The map embed is the only possible third-party content—or has been replaced with a link.
* [ ] Toast and Uber Eats are ordinary outbound links, not embedded tracking widgets.
* [ ] The hosting provider’s basic security features and HTTPS are enabled.

A short privacy page is optional. If you include one, it should say only what is actually true—for example, that the restaurant does not directly collect personal information through the website and that external ordering services have their own privacy practices.

### Basic footer

* [ ] Restaurant name
* [ ] Current year
* [ ] Copyright notice, such as `© 2026 Restaurant Name`
* [ ] Phone number or contact method
* [ ] Links to both locations
* [ ] Optional accessibility contact
* [ ] Optional privacy page

A copyright notice is useful but not required for copyright protection.

## You probably do not need

* [ ] No elaborate Terms of Service page
* [ ] No cookie banner, provided there truly are no nonessential cookies or tracking tools
* [ ] No dietary disclaimer solely because the menu contains meat or other ingredients
* [ ] No account deletion policy
* [ ] No refund policy on the informational website, since purchases occur through Toast or Uber Eats
* [ ] No HIPAA notice
* [ ] No federal calorie labels solely because there are two restaurant locations
* [ ] No “we are not responsible for anything” disclaimer

The three things I would take most seriously are **accessibility, permission to use all images/content, and keeping the menu, hours and ordering links accurate**. This is a practical U.S. checklist rather than jurisdiction-specific legal advice.
